# Bounded correction and recovery in Story generation

Status: accepted as amended by [ADR 0006](0006-persistent-review-and-incremental-story-blueprints.md), which supersedes fresh substantive reviewer sessions per correction, hard semantic correction-count exhaustion, and semantic finding identity matching for ordinary review progression. Other recovery, authorization, integrity and protocol decisions remain applicable.

## Context

Story specialists sometimes make correctable mistakes or identify obligations that require another specialist's authority. The existing controller can block safely, but repeated human-triggered restarts and rigid stage handoffs are not adequate recovery for autonomous Story work. Treating a reviewed assignment as permanently unchangeable also prevents legitimate downstream corrections.

## Decision

**A failed assignment is a repair finding, not automatically a failed Turn.** Prefer a hybrid correction policy: the original specialist receives bounded feedback for defects within its existing authority, while cross-domain defects, missing prerequisites, and corrections to earlier reviewed work return to the controller/orchestrator for a narrowly authorized corrective assignment. Previously reviewed work may be superseded by new independently validated and reviewed commits; preserve the original immutable evidence and reconsider affected downstream dependencies. A model or reviewer may report missing work but cannot confer write permission, expand protected scope, or approve its own changes.

**Repair attempts are finite and attributable.** Track persistent, source-bound repair findings so differently worded reports of the same underlying defect do not reset budgets. Expose configurable limits by category (local corrections, dependency repair cycles, transient infrastructure retries) and a Turn-wide ceiling. A correction attempt counts when a specialist submits a completed revision for verification, even if verification rejects it—not when an API request fails before producing a result. Reconcile uncertain completed operations before counting or retrying; a specialist's report that the defect is outside its authority routes to orchestration instead of repeated local writes. Initial defaults are two local corrections, three dependency-repair cycles per finding, two transient-infrastructure retries, and a Turn-wide ceiling of 12 completed agentic correction attempts (independent of transient infrastructure retries). This ceiling is a safety limit, not a target; the controller can escalate earlier for stagnation. Counters survive process restarts and checkpoint recovery, and an author may explicitly authorize further attempts without clearing the previous ledger. The controller distinguishes automatic repair in progress, genuine author input/approval, exhausted repair budgets, and failures of evidence integrity. Reaching a limit preserves eligible completed work and invites guided continuation; untrustworthy evidence fails closed without automatic replay. The handling of exhausted or explicitly extended budgets must preserve the existing ledger.

**Findings remain identifiable across revisions.** Assign a persistent Repair finding identity based on the violated Story obligation, relevant target records, and defect category; error prose is not the identity and a changed commit alone does not reset its budget. Each attempt preserves its evidence and source revision. The controller independently checks whether a purportedly new finding is actually a continuation of an existing defect.

**Revalidate affected dependents rather than discarding them.** When a corrective commit changes an earlier result, identify downstream consumers and causal dependents from authenticated source/assignment relationships. Mark affected work for revalidation, run deterministic checks and required independent semantic reviews against the new revision, and authorize corrections only when those checks show an actual problem. Unaffected reviewed work carries forward; immutable historical reviews and commits are not rewritten.

**Autonomous corrections remain bounded by the original author objective.** The controller may authorize needed Events, Facts, or revisions to existing scenes within approved scope and production limits. Optional enrichment cannot use a defect as a pretext to widen the Story. Protected author decisions and material scope changes still require the appropriate explicit approval.

**Reconcile interrupted writers before retrying.** Check the persistent work-item receipt, Git state, and recorded tool operations before deciding whether an interrupted action committed, can safely resume its original Pi session, or requires a new explicitly authorized assignment. Uncertain partial edits are preserved as recovery evidence, not blindly replayed. Immutable reviewed checkpoint proofs are evaluated against their historical committed source, separately from the latest unfinished working-tree draft.

**Escalate stagnation rather than repeat ineffective edits.** Compare each completed correction against the original finding, source changes, and independent review evidence. Distinguish partial progress from unrelated changes or oscillation between previously rejected states. The controller may return the defect to orchestration for a revised strategy before exhausting the local correction budget; escalation never resets the finding identity or Turn-wide budget.

**Permit bounded review disputes without self-approval.** If the specialist provides source-linked evidence that a reviewer finding is incorrect, the controller checks deterministic facts first. A genuinely semantic dispute goes to a fresh independent review of both claims and the actual sources, subject to bounded review attempts. Neither agent's argument alone can override a sealed failure; unresolved disagreement escalates rather than generating an endless debate.

**Coordinate cross-specialist corrections through versioned plans.** For a finding spanning multiple domains, the orchestrator proposes a source-bound corrective plan with precise assignments and causal dependencies. The trusted controller separately validates the plan, grants bounded authority and schedules specialists in causal order; the orchestrator does not confer write grants or initiate unrestricted agent-to-agent writing. A repair that causes a distinct downstream defect creates a separately tracked Repair finding linked to its originating correction, consuming the same Turn-wide ceiling. After each correction, the controller first revalidates affected previously completed work, carries forward anything still valid without another writer call, and dispatches new corrections only for demonstrated failures. Prevent cascading work from turning into an unbounded expansion of the original author objective.

**Findings are proposals until the controller verifies them.** A specialist or reviewer reports the violated requirement, source evidence, affected records and suggested correction. The controller checks deterministic claims and authenticated source facts before treating the finding as repairable; the orchestrator interprets semantic uncertainty and proposes the plan, subject to separate controller authorization. A mistaken specialist diagnosis must not create a write grant or force needless work.

**Repair ordinary mistakes without unnecessary author interruption.** Autonomous and Collaborative modes both repair routine structural, technical and editorial defects automatically. Author input is reserved for genuinely consequential creative choices, protected decisions, material scope changes or exhausted recovery paths. New author instructions take priority at the next safe execution boundary over scheduling another repair attempt: settle or safely interrupt and reconcile any active writer, reassess unresolved findings against the revised objective, and preserve verified work that still applies.

**Exhaustion is a safe, resumable stop—not successful completion.** When corrections or disputes exhaust their authorized budget, retain the Turn, its full evidence and eligible completed work. Show the unresolved obligations and prior attempts; permit author guidance, explicit budget extension, or an authorized objective revision followed by continuation in the same Thread. Never waive mandatory acceptance criteria to declare success.

**Resource limits supplement correctness limits.** Track configurable consumption and execution-time ceilings independently of correction-attempt counts. A budget pause at a safe boundary does not discard validated work or consume remaining correction attempts; author-authorized increases preserve previous usage. The existing job-level Pi budget controller already enforces steps, model calls, priced cost where available and active seconds, and reports token usage. Integrate repair admission with that controller rather than establishing a second inconsistent resource meter; a hard token ceiling is not currently part of the enforced `JobLimits` shape.

**Persist repair state separately from canonical Story content.** A controller-owned Repair ledger in private job state records findings, source revisions, assigned specialists, bounded attempts, review and dispute outcomes, dependency impacts, and budgets. Link its immutable identities and receipts to LangGraph state transitions and verified Git commits. Git remains authoritative for canonical Story content; Pi sessions remain conversation evidence, not execution authority.

**Reconcile every interrupted correction idempotently.** Persist assignment admission, writer invocation, draft completion, validation, commit and review phases. On restart, compare receipts, the Pi transcript, Git HEAD and working-tree changes before completing an interrupted operation, safely continuing its original session, or authorizing a fresh continuation. Repeating reconciliation must not duplicate commits or double-count attempts. Uncertain unfinished edits remain protected evidence until resolved.

**Serialize Story mutations; allow safe independent reads.** Keep canonical writing and commit publication under the existing job writer lock, with queued corrective assignments verified against the Story state when admitted for execution. Independent read-only planning or review can overlap where safe, but no separate writer may advance a job's worktree while a correction is active. Separate Threads have their own worktrees and branches. Once a finding is confirmed, prioritize its corrective assignment at the next safe boundary ahead of work depending on the affected records; unrelated pending work need not be cancelled, but should not needlessly delay repair. Authorize corrective execution against the job's actual current HEAD, checking its source and dependency assumptions then. Sequential commits by the same orchestrator can legitimately advance HEAD between a finding and dispatch; unexpected changes while a writer holds the mutation lane are integrity anomalies, not routine rebasing cases.

**Preserve job-wide resource limits and Turn-attributed repair limits.** The controller maintains the separate Turn correction ceiling, with per-finding budgets, alongside existing cumulative job resource limits for calls and active time. Attribute resource usage to Turns for visibility without resetting job usage when the author starts another Turn. Reuse the existing Pi budget ledger rather than adding an independent source of truth.

**Keep Pi histories independent of repair authority.** A specialist continues its original Pi conversation across bounded local corrections when the session can be safely resumed, including feedback from each failed rereview. Each revised source is assessed in a fresh, independent read-only reviewer Pi session—not by continuing the preceding reviewer's conversation. The reviewer receives the original assignment, source-linked finding, prior failed verdicts and correction attempts, relevant diffs, and current source evidence, so it can judge the attempted fix without relying on another agent's private chat history. The controller's persistent findings, attempt ledger, and review receipts—not either conversation history—govern approval, authority, and exhaustion. Preserve the original Pi session and its cache-friendly history; NovelTea must not add proactive or per-attempt compaction. Leave Pi SDK's built-in automatic threshold/overflow compaction enabled and use its normal session continuation. In the installed @earendil-works/pi-coding-agent 1.5.0, this defaults to a 16,384-token context reserve and 20,000 tokens of recent context kept after compaction; overflow recovery attempts one compact-and-retry. Compaction entries and full transcript history remain persisted in Pi's session manager. If Pi cannot continue even after its own bounded recovery, preserve the transcript and hand off to a fresh authorized specialist session with source-bound repair evidence; such a context-failure handoff does not reset finding counts or itself consume a completed correction attempt.

**Represent recoverable execution explicitly.** Keep a running Turn active while a finding progresses through correction, revalidation, review or replanning; do not publish a temporary `blocked` result for automatic repair. Expose a concise recovery status and only terminally stop automated progress on exhaustion or integrity failure (with distinct reasons). Author and budget pauses remain resumable with their actual required action.

**Show useful recovery activity without flooding Story Chat.** Provide compact progress and attempt counts within execution activity, with the underlying source-linked findings, revisions and review history available for inspection. Routine successful corrections do not create repeated conversational alerts; genuinely exhausted or unsafe work presents a clear action to the author.

**Continue exhausted repairs in the same Thread.** After author guidance or an explicit additional allowance, keep the existing Story job, receipts, eligible work, original counts, and transcripts. Recheck whether the guidance modifies authorization or objective scope before scheduling a new bounded corrective assignment. An objective change passes through normal Turn planning; exhaustion never silently becomes successful completion.

**Treat failed reviewer execution separately from a negative review.** Invalid output format, missing mandatory reads, provider failures or malformed evidence are reviewer execution failures, not evidence of a writer defect. Attempt bounded same-session protocol correction where safe, or re-run a fresh read-only review of immutable sources; never convert a failed or unavailable verdict into a pass or spend the writer's correction budget for reviewer execution failures.

**Keep job-pinned model selection.** Do not automatically change provider/model because repairs stagnate. Prefer a new authorized corrective strategy; if changing the model is desirable, require explicit authorization at a safe boundary, preserve prior source evidence and record the new model selection separately.

**Unify protocol-level failures across graph roles.** Classify failures by cause, rather than by specialist role. Malformed JSON, mismatched response schemas, missing mandatory response fields and omitted mandatory reads use reusable protocol-correction policies supplied with task-specific validators. Provider outages use infrastructure retries; incorrect Story content uses the separate semantic repair lifecycle; ambiguous filesystem operations require reconciliation; untrustworthy source or authority evidence fails closed. For recoverable invalid responses, allow at most two additional complete response attempts after the initial failure, preferably within the same active Pi conversation with exact validation feedback. Count them as protocol retries, not semantic corrections or against the Turn's twelve agentic correction attempts; all model calls still consume ordinary job resources. During a format-only correction, remove writing tools so the agent can fix its report without altering already-authored Story content; mandatory source-inspection corrections may use only authorized read tools. Validate the resulting report independently against canonical files and preserved receipts; a formatting retry cannot clear, soften, or argumentatively override a negative reviewer finding. Exhaustion yields a typed `protocol-exhausted` outcome for that invocation, not an assertion that the Story itself failed. After exhausting three total responses in one invocation, the controller may authorize at most one fresh-session protocol recovery invocation for that same operation, subject to ordinary budget admission. Read-only roles receive the original assignment and immutable source evidence; writer roles must first reconcile completed edits, and the fresh session may complete the missing report but must not blindly repeat writing. The fresh invocation has the same two additional response attempts. Another exhaustion stops the operation with diagnostic evidence and no further automatic session replacement. Shared validation feedback names the exact offending contract paths, expected shapes and relevant source constraints; sanitize it to exclude credentials and internal exception stacks, and never use it to grant authority. Persist a per-invocation response-contract identity, attempt count and Pi session linkage before further prompts; after an interruption, reconcile actual completed assistant responses and receipts so neither a graph replay nor a restart resets counts or sends a duplicate response-repair prompt.

**Integrate recovery into the production generation graph.** Use typed controller-owned recovery dispositions and transitions for local correction, dependency replanning, author input, budget exhaustion and integrity failure. Recoverable findings must route through correction, independent verification and back to ordinary scheduling without terminating the Turn. Specialist-specific repair implementations are bounded adapters to one shared recovery lifecycle, not separate execution engines.

**Preflight structural dependencies before dispatch.** Check known canonical prerequisites, verified source contracts and assignment evidence before paying for a specialist. A missing prerequisite becomes a Repair finding and a corrective assignment for the responsible domain, rather than an impossible downstream write. Semantic defects not statically discoverable still enter the same recovery lifecycle when reported.

**Completion requires discharged obligations, not just an empty schedule.** Accept the current verified Story revision only when the author objective and its acceptance criteria are met, no required Repair findings or affected downstream revalidations remain unresolved, mandatory native and independent semantic checks pass, and no required author choices or protected approvals are pending. Recheck after corrective commits and plan amendments; finishing every listed specialist assignment alone does not establish completion.

**Certify recovery deterministically before full live Story generation.** Exercise local repair, cross-specialist prerequisite repair, mistaken reviewer findings, stagnant or oscillating corrections, interrupted writes, persisted restart, exhausted budgets and source-proof tampering with failure-injection fixtures. Verify both eventual progress and refusal of unsafe actions. Use a small number of real-Pi scenarios after deterministic gates pass, and defer expensive full autonomous certification until then.

**Minimize added machinery.** The recovery design's scheduling/preflight, specialist execution, verification/review, progress update and recovery decision are logical responsibilities, not a requirement to replace the current generation graph with five physical nodes. Extend its existing nodes and specialist adapters, prefer one shared protocol retry helper and a thin indexed repair ledger referencing existing trusted receipts, and use existing inventory/dependency proofs before introducing another persistent model. An additional physical recovery-decision node is justified only if it centralizes typed routing; do not implement a parallel recovery graph, per-failure state machines, or duplicated checkpoint ownership. Preserve compatibility with already checkpointed generation threads through explicit schema/version handling.

## Consequences

The orchestrator must be able to schedule bounded corrective work against earlier verified stages without rewriting checkpoint history. Recovery and graph transitions must represent repairable conditions separately from hard integrity blocks, and deterministic tests must cover bounded retry, escalation, carry-forward, and interrupted work.

# Goal-driven Story orchestration within NovelTea Studio

Status: accepted; confirmed during the joint `noveltea-gen` / `noveltea-skills` design review.

## Context

Story generation currently starts from fixed full-Story stages, while focused revisions have different planning rules and cannot create missing supporting records. Separately, `noveltea-skills` contains `nt-story-generate` and `nt-story-collaborate` plus an experimental standalone collaborative scheduler. This duplicates orchestration concepts, makes new versus existing Story behavior inconsistent, and risks freezing a plausible initial plan even when specialist work reveals new dependencies.

## Decision

**One goal-driven orchestrator, one production execution graph.** Rename and revise `nt-story-generate` as `nt-story-orchestrate`. It interprets an author's objective against verified canonical Story state, develops a provisional work plan, selects appropriate narrative specialists, revises remaining work at safe boundaries, and judges completion against the requested outcome. It handles both complete Story generation (for example, "make me a mystery game") and bounded changes to an existing Story. The NovelTea Studio LangGraph/controller is the only supported production execution engine: it owns job and Thread lifecycle, work authorization, Git history, writer locks, deterministic validation, reviewer gates, persistence, recovery, and scheduling. A skill or model proposal does not confer write authority.

**Baseline discovery is part of the agentic flow, not a separate user workflow.** Before generating a new Story, the orchestrator invokes the `nt-story-discovery` methodology to establish a minimally coherent Creative Direction, presents necessary questions with recommendations, and obtains exact author approval of the foundation. This applies in **both** Autonomous and Collaborative modes; Autonomous mode alone does not waive the initial interview. If the author explicitly delegates all unresolved decisions and says not to ask questions, the agent may fill in those choices and present the proposed baseline directly, still subject to the existing review/approval safeguards. An approved baseline resumes the original objective automatically; the author does not have to request generation a second time. Existing verified foundations and settled decisions are reused rather than re-interviewed. Reuse the provenances, proposed-foundation checks, and approval controls from existing onboarding implementation, integrated as graph steps in normal Story Chat, without retaining a competing onboarding wizard or discovery pipeline.

**Creative modes affect participation, not the graph.** `nt-story-collaborate` becomes the portable author-interaction methodology for Collaborative mode, alongside `nt-story-orchestrate` for planning. Collaborative work elicits consequential creative decisions with specific recommendations, accepts partial answers and delegation, and does not interrupt for routine repairs. Autonomous work resolves routine delegated creative questions after baseline discovery. Both modes respect protected canon and controller scope; mode changes do not alter the original objective, automatically approve decisions, or import another Thread's unpublished changes. No parallel autonomous/collaborative generation scheduler is maintained.

**Plans evolve as verified work emerges.** A work plan is a versioned, revisable collection of specialist assignments, prerequisites, intended outcomes, and completion checks—not a mandatory sequence or a grant of authority. Top-down narrative stages are useful dependency knowledge for full-Story creation, but are not mandatory for focused work. After a completed specialist cluster, relevant reviewer finding, author instruction, or reported dependency, the orchestrator may add, remove, reorder, narrow, or replace pending assignments. It may autonomously authorize *causally necessary* supporting work, including creation of new canonical records, when the trusted controller validates the revised bounded scope and protection rules. Optional unrelated enrichment is deferred. Material changes to the author's requested goal or protected decisions require the applicable explicit approval. Preserve verified results when dependency/evidence checks permit carry-forward, and reconcile ambiguous interrupted writes rather than automatically replaying them. A new author instruction normally takes effect at the next safe work boundary in the same Thread, not after the entire prior generation plan terminates.

**Shared knowledge, bounded writes.** Every specialist may consult the `nt-catalog` canonical reference and inspect relevant actual Story records across domains. It may read adjacent craft-specialist guidance when needed and report typed missing prerequisites to the orchestrator. Only its controller-approved paths/types may be changed. Read access to an adjacent skill does not delegate that specialist's authority, and normal specialists do not become orchestrators themselves. Make orchestrator invocation explicit, including `policy.allow_implicit_invocation: false` in supported OpenAI metadata and `disable-model-invocation: true` in applicable Pi skill frontmatter, while enforcing role isolation in NovelTea Gen's skill loader; invocation metadata is guidance, never a security boundary. Protect author-only noncanonical inbox material from ordinary discovery. An independent reviewer remains read-only.

**Completion is depth-dependent.** Preserve the requested outcome's breadth, target, and delivery depth as completion criteria rather than imposing a fixed sequence. An outline, interactive structure, and complete player-facing narrative have different acceptance obligations. Earlier example depth names (`main-milestones`, `all-milestones`, `interactive-structure`, `full-narrative`) are conceptual levels, not a requirement to revive obsolete preset identifiers. A broad request for a complete game must not stop at a valid outline or mechanical record-count target.

**External source import remains separate.** The portable `nt-story-import` skill handles manuscript and notes extraction/conversion methodology; Studio's future source-aware, resumable import flow is not part of this generation-orchestrator refactor and must not be silently folded into the normal generation graph. It may reuse the same trusted Story and validation infrastructure. This is distinct from the forbidden implicit import of another Thread's unpublished changes.

**Skills stay portable; the standalone collaborative scheduler is historical.** Retain and improve reusable `nt-catalog`, craft, review, orchestration, and collaborative guidance for agent hosts outside Studio. The separate `noveltea-skills` `pnpm collaborate` / `collaborative-workflow.ts` / `collaborative-pi.ts` implementation is obsolete test/development infrastructure: mine it for reusable techniques and evaluations, but do not integrate, support, or preserve it as an alternative production scheduler.

## Consequences and implementation boundary

First audit the actual skill dependencies and shared craft knowledge; do not inadvertently remove cross-domain understanding. Consolidate first-Turn and follow-up intent/plan entrypoints; let the orchestrator choose full-Story completion, bounded new-record creation, or focused revision from the objective and trusted Story state. Add typed plan amendments, bounded authorization and carry-forward receipts, safe-boundary replanning, goal-specific stop criteria, and inline baseline discovery/review. Reuse existing sandbox, native CLI, protected-canon approvals, Git checkpoint, and independent semantic-review safeguards rather than rewriting them.

Certify both repositories in tandem using the same behavioral cases: sparse "make me a mystery game" with and without explicit delegation; Collaborative baseline interview and approval; existing Story with a verified foundation; focused dialogue needing new Character/Relationship/Event context; a specialist discovering an unforeseen dependency; unnecessary pending work being dropped; a live author direction change; an interrupted or rejected writer; cross-domain clue/knowledge continuity; protected-canon conflicts; independent editorial review; and successful, goal-bounded completion. Fixture/controller tests establish safety; completed real-Pi tests and reviewed Story output establish actual agent behavior. Retire or rewrite stale skill-routing and full-Story-only evaluation assumptions, keeping historical evidence intact.

Implementation sequencing and issue ownership belong in the issue tracker rather than a second duplicate project roadmap.
